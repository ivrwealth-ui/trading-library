# 1. What Is Momentum Trading?

## Buying strength, not weakness

**Momentum trading** is the discipline of buying assets that are *already rising* and selling them when they *stop rising* — the opposite of bargain-hunting. Where a value investor asks "what is cheap?", the momentum trader asks "what is strong, and is that strength likely to continue?"

The central premise is simple and empirically supported: **assets that have outperformed recently tend to continue outperforming in the near term, and assets that have lagged tend to keep lagging.** Momentum is the observation that *strength persists* — not forever, but more often than chance would predict, and long enough to profit from.

## Momentum vs. its cousins

It is worth placing momentum trading precisely among the other approaches in this series:

- **Momentum trading** (this book) — short-to-medium horizon (weeks to a few months), driven by *price action and trend*, using technical signals like breakouts and moving averages.
- **Momentum investing** (companion book) — longer horizon (months to years), driven by the *momentum factor* in academic finance, using trailing-return ranking and low turnover.
- **Swing trading** (companion book) — multi-day to a few weeks, capturing individual price "swings," combining both momentum (continuation) and mean-reversion tactics.
- **Mean reversion** — the *opposite* bet: that an extreme move will snap back. Momentum traders are, by definition, betting *against* snap-backs.

The boundaries blur, but the distinction matters: momentum trading is fundamentally about **identifying an existing trend and participating in its continuation**.

## The core beliefs of a momentum trader

Momentum trading rests on three working assumptions:

1. **Trends persist.** A market in an uptrend is more likely to keep rising than to reverse — the behavioural and structural forces behind this are Chapter 2's subject.
2. **New highs are information, not danger.** A stock making a new high is often *strong*, not overpriced. The momentum trader reads a new high as demand overwhelming supply.
3. **You don't need to predict the turn.** Momentum trading does not try to buy the bottom or sell the top. It tries to *capture the middle* of a move — entering after a trend confirms and exiting when it breaks.

The last point is the soul of the approach: **momentum traders sacrifice the first part of a move (waiting for confirmation) and the last part (exiting on the reversal signal) to capture the reliable middle.** This is a deliberate trade-off — lower win rate, but larger average winners than losers.

## The anatomy of a momentum trade

Every momentum trade, regardless of the specific strategy, has the same three parts:

1. **Entry** — triggered by a *signal that strength is present*: a breakout to a new high, a moving-average crossover, or a pullback that holds.
2. **Hold** — as long as the trend *remains intact* (above the trend filter, making higher highs). No prediction; just observation.
3. **Exit** — triggered by a *signal that strength has failed*: a break of the trend, a reversal below a stop, or a time-based rule.

This "enter on strength, exit on weakness" structure is what all five strategies in this book share. They differ only in *how* they measure strength and *how* they define failure.

## What momentum trading is not

- It is **not** day trading — momentum trading holds positions for weeks, not minutes.
- It is **not** "chasing" — chasing is buying *after* a move is already extended, with no plan. Momentum trading buys on a *defined signal*, with a *defined exit* and a *defined risk*.
- It is **not** a guarantee — momentum fails periodically, and the failures can be sharp. The risk management in Chapter 9 exists precisely because momentum strategies lose money in choppy, trendless markets.

## Summary

- Momentum trading buys strength and sells weakness — the opposite of bargain-hunting.
- It rests on the persistence of trends and treats new highs as information.
- It captures the middle of a move: enter on confirmation, exit on failure.
- Every momentum trade has three parts: entry, hold, exit.
- It is a defined, rules-based discipline — not chasing, and not prediction.
