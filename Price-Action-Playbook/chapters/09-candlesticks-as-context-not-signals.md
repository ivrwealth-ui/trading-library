# 9. Candlesticks as Context, Not Signals

Candlesticks are the most over-used and mis-used part of price action. A hammer is not a signal; it is a *description of a single bar*. It becomes meaningful only in context.

## The right way to use candles

1. **A candle confirms a level's reaction.** A long-wick rejection bar *at a major support* means sellers tried and were absorbed. The *same* bar in the middle of a range means almost nothing.
2. **A candle describes the fight, not the winner.** The body shows who controlled the period; the wicks show where the extremes were rejected. Read both.
3. **Context is everything.** The most reliable candle patterns — pin bars, engulfing bars, inside bars — are reliable only at the *right location* (a tested level, an opening-range edge, a value-area extreme).

## The candle you should watch most in the Indian session

The **inside bar** (a bar whose range is entirely inside the previous bar's range) deserves special note: it is the fingerprint of **compression**, and compression precedes expansion. A cluster of inside bars at a level is a coiled spring — the breakout from an inside-bar cluster (especially after the midday lull) is often one of the cleanest continuation setups on the NSE.
