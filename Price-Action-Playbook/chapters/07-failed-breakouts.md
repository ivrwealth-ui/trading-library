# 7. Failed Breakouts

The failed breakout is often cited as one of the most profitable and reliable price-action patterns — because it reveals exactly where one side is trapped, and trapped traders are the fuel for the next move.

## The anatomy of a failed breakout

1. Price approaches a well-defined level (a swing high, a range boundary, an opening-range edge).
2. Price **breaks** the level — it trades beyond it, drawing in breakout buyers (or sellers).
3. Price **fails** to follow through and, within a few bars, reverses back *through* the level into the range.

The traders who entered on the breakout are now wrong and underwater. Their stop-losses sit just beyond the level. When price re-crosses the level, those stops are triggered — and the stop-running becomes the momentum that drives the reversal.

## The two classic failed-breakout setups

- **The Spring** (failed breakdown / shakeout). Price breaks *below* a support level, traps sellers, and snaps back above it. The reversal is bullish. This is the classic "stop run" under a support.
- **The Upthrust** (failed breakout / bull trap). Price breaks *above* a resistance level, traps buyers, and snaps back below it. The reversal is bearish.

## How to tell a real breakout from a fake (before the fact)

You cannot know for certain in advance, but the odds tilt heavily based on **context**:

- **Where is the breakout?** Breakouts from a *tight balance* at the start of a move tend to have the best odds of following through. Breakouts from a *late, stretched, heavily tested* level (or into the midday lull) tend to fail most often.
- **What is the character of the break?** A clean break with expanding range and volume = genuine. A sloppy, marginal, one-bar poke with no participation = suspect.
- **What does the higher timeframe say?** A breakout *with* the daily trend is more likely to hold than one *against* it. Counter-trend breakouts tend to fail more often.

## The failed-breakout checklist

- The level is clear and well-defined.
- Price breaks it, then **fails to hold** and re-enters the range within a handful of bars.
- The reversal is **swift** (a slow drift back is less convincing than a fast snap-back).
- The setup has a defined reference for risk (the extreme of the failed probe) and a target (the opposite side of the range, or the next value area).
