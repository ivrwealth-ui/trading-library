# 1. What Price Action Actually Is

Price action is the practice of reading a market directly from its **price, time and volume**, with the chart stripped of lagging indicators. The premise is simple and powerful: every piece of information that matters — news, earnings, flows, sentiment — is already reflected in the price. The price is not a *result* to be explained; it is the *record* of what participants actually did, in real time, with real money.

Three ideas sit at the centre of all price action:

1. **Price moves in auctions.** Every market is an ongoing negotiation between buyers and sellers. When buyers are more aggressive, price rises; when sellers are, price falls. Price stops where the two sides reach temporary agreement — and that is where the interesting levels form.
2. **Structure tells you who is in control.** The sequence of higher highs and higher lows (or lower highs and lower lows) is the market's own confession of which side is winning. You do not need an indicator to see a trend; you need to read the structure.
3. **Levels are memories.** Prices that were rejected once tend to be remembered. A level that produced a sharp reversal is a place where participants have a stake — and where they will defend or attack again.

This book is organised as a playbook: first the foundations (structure, the Indian session, auctions), then the setups (levels, failed breakouts, break-and-retest), then a working process you can run every day.

## Why price action suits the Indian market

The NSE is unusually well-suited to this approach for three reasons. First, the session is a **single block** (09:15–15:30 IST) with no lunch break, so the auction develops uninterrupted. Second, the market is **open-heavy** — a large share of the day's range and volume tends to come in the first hour, which makes the opening auction decisive. Third, Nifty and BankNifty are deep, liquid instruments whose levels are respected by institutional participants the world over.
