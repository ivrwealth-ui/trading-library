# 3. The Indian Session

Price action is not the same at every hour. The Indian market has a distinct daily rhythm, and a level that means one thing at 09:20 means another at 14:30.

## The session clock (IST)

| Time | Phase | What happens |
|---|---|---|
| 09:00–09:15 | Pre-open | Orders are collected and matched at a single opening price; no continuous trading |
| 09:15–09:30 | Opening range | The first 15 minutes of continuous trading set the opening range; highest volatility |
| 09:30–11:00 | Morning session | The initial balance (first hour, 09:15–10:15) completes; the opening range is accepted or rejected |
| 11:00–13:30 | Midday | Typically lower volume; often a period of balance/compression |
| 13:30–15:15 | Afternoon session | Volume and directional energy often return |
| 15:15–15:35 | Closing auction (F&O stocks) | End-of-day positioning; the auction price sets the official close |

Since 3 August 2026, stocks with F&O contracts stop continuous trading at 15:15 and move to NSE's Closing Auction Session, whose equilibrium price becomes the closing price. Other stocks trade continuously until 15:30 and keep the VWAP-based close. Index and stock F&O trade until 15:40, and the post-close session runs 15:50–16:00.

## The opening range

The **opening range** (commonly the first 15 minutes, 09:15–09:30, though some traders use 09:15–10:00) is the single most important reference of the day. It is the market's first statement of value for the session.

**How to read the opening range:**

- **Opening-range breakout.** A clean break above the opening-range high with *follow-through* (not a one-bar poke) is a classic intraday continuation signal; a break below the low is its mirror.
- **Opening-range failure.** A break that quickly reverses back *inside* the range is a failed breakout (Chapter 7) and is often the better trade — the market just showed you where the trapped traders are.
- **Initial balance and value.** The initial balance is the first hour's range (09:15–10:15). Across the session, watch whether price spends time *inside* the opening range and the initial balance (acceptance) or *away* from them (rejection). Where price spends time is where value is being established.

## Session context matters

The same setup is not worth the same risk at every hour. High-conviction moves tend to set up in the **first hour** and in the **last hour**. The midday lull is where breakouts most often fail — there is simply less participation to sustain them. A simple rule that many traders find useful: *be most aggressive in the auction hours (open and close) and most patient in the middle.*
