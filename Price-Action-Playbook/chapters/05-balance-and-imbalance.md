# 5. Balance and Imbalance

Markets spend far more time in **balance** than in trend. Understanding the cycle between the two is the difference between trading a trend and getting chopped up in a range.

## Balance (rotation)

Balance is a market in a range: buyers and sellers are evenly matched, price rotates between two boundaries, and value is well-defined. In balance, **breakouts usually fail** and **mean-reversion works** — price is attracted back to value.

**How to read balance:**

- Identify the upper and lower boundary (the extremes of rotation).
- Note that in balance, the edges are the trade locations: fading the extremes back toward value, with tight risk.
- Recognise when balance is *too* tight — compression is the precursor to expansion.

## Imbalance (trend)

Imbalance is a market moving directionally: one side is clearly in control, value is migrating, and pullbacks are shallow and bought (or sold). In imbalance, **breakouts work** and **pullbacks are the entry**, not the reversal.

**How to read imbalance:**

- Value is migrating (higher POCs day over day in an uptrend, lower in a downtrend).
- Pullbacks hold the previous swing low (uptrend) or previous swing high (downtrend).
- Breakouts of minor levels get follow-through rather than failure.

## The transition

The most valuable skill in price action is catching the **moment balance breaks into imbalance** — and catching the **failed attempt** to break (which keeps you in balance). The sequence to watch:

1. Price compresses (balance tightens).
2. Price probes a boundary and either **accepts** (breaks and holds → imbalance) or **rejects** (fails and snaps back → balance, and a failed breakout).
3. The *follow-through after the probe* is the tell. A one-bar poke means nothing; sustained trade away from the level means everything.
