# 11. Risk and Trade Management

No setup in this book matters more than how you manage the risk around it. Price action gives you an edge at the *margins*; risk management is what lets that edge compound instead of being blown up by one bad session.

## The fundamentals

- **Risk a defined amount per trade.** Decide your risk (commonly a small fixed percentage of capital) *before* entry, and size the position so that the stop distance translates to that risk.
- **Place stops where the idea is wrong, not where it is comfortable.** For a failed breakout, the stop belongs beyond the extreme of the failed probe. For a break-and-retest, it belongs beyond the retested level. If the stop is too far, the setup is not tradeable at your size — pass.
- **Think in R-multiples.** Measure every outcome as a multiple of your initial risk (R). A trader's edge is expressed as the average R per trade across many trades. Tracking R removes the emotion from any single win or loss.

## The R-multiple mindset

| Outcome | Meaning |
|---|---|
| +1R | You made your risk once over |
| −1R | You lost your risk |
| +2R / +3R | A solid win |
| −2R | A stop that gapped or slipped past your level |

**How to read your own performance:** a trader can be profitable with a win rate under 50% if the average win is larger than the average loss. The R-multiple is the honest accounting that reveals whether your edge is real. It is the single most important habit in this book.

## When to stand down

- When the market is in a **tight, low-volume midday balance** with no clear level nearby.
- When you have taken **two consecutive losses** — the market is telling you your read is off; stop and reassess rather than revenge-trade.
- On **event days** (see Book 4, *Event & Earnings Trading on the NSE*) where the gap and volatility make levels unreliable.
- When you cannot articulate, in one sentence, *why* the trade is valid and *where* it is wrong. If you cannot, there is no trade.
