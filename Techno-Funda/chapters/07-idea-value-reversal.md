# 7. Idea 3 — Value + Reversal Timing

## Cheap businesses, bought only when the tide turns

The first two ideas buy *strength*. This one buys *cheapness* — but with the crucial techno-funda twist: a cheap stock is only bought once the price shows a **reversal**, because cheap alone is a trap.

## The idea

Value investing's great enemy is the **value trap**: a stock that is cheap for a reason, and stays cheap (or gets cheaper) for years. The classic mistake is to buy a low-P/E stock simply *because* it is low-P/E, and then watch it drift down while the "cheapness" deepens.

The Value + Reversal Timing idea keeps the value screen but adds a non-negotiable gate: **the technical reversal.** A cheap stock becomes a *candidate* only when the price stops falling and starts turning up. You are buying value, but you are *waiting for the market to stop punishing it* first.

## The fundamental screen (what)

- **Low valuation** — e.g., P/E below its own 5-year median, P/B below 2 (or sector-relative), a reasonable PEG.
- **Still profitable** — positive earnings (a value screen should exclude loss-makers; those are a different bet).
- **A reason to recover** — healthy balance sheet (low debt), stable or recovering margins, a catalyst on the horizon (earnings inflection, new management, sector cycle).

The screen identifies *cheap but viable* businesses — not distressed ones. Distress is excluded because it is a different (and far riskier) game.

## The technical trigger (when)

1. **A base or bottom is forming** — the decline has slowed, price is no longer making lower lows.
2. **The reversal** — the first confirmed signs of a turn: a **higher low**, followed by a **close above a short-term MA (e.g., the 20-day or 50-day)** on rising volume. Some versions require a close above the 200-day MA before acting.
3. **Stop** — below the reversal low (the higher low). If price falls below it, the "reversal" was false and the value trap is still in force.
4. **Exit** — trend break (below the 50/200-day MA) or the stop; the position is held only as long as the recovery continues.

## A worked example (an NSE cyclical/cheap name)

- A large-cap in a beaten-down sector trades at a P/E well below its 5-year median, with a clean balance sheet and early signs of an earnings inflection. It passes the value screen.
- The stock has been falling for a year but recently **stopped making new lows**, building a base between ₹180 and ₹200.
- It **makes a higher low at ₹195, then closes above its 50-day MA at ₹210 on rising volume**. **Entry** at ₹212.
- **Stop** at ₹193 (below the higher low).
- The recovery continues as the sector cycle turns; the stock climbs to ₹280. The investor holds while the uptrend persists, exiting on a trend break at ₹265.

Result: the investor bought a cheap stock *only after* the market confirmed the turn — avoiding the years of pain that a pure value investor would have absorbed.

## Risks and limitations

- **False reversals.** A reversal signal can fail — the "turn" was just a bounce in a continuing downtrend. The stop below the higher low caps this.
- **Late to genuine recoveries.** Waiting for confirmation means missing the very bottom; the first, sharpest leg of a V-shaped recovery is given up.
- **The value trap persists.** A stock can look "reversing" and then resume its decline; the screen must also have been right that the business is *viable*, not just cheap.
- **Catalyst dependence.** Many cheap stocks never re-rate without a specific catalyst; the screen's "reason to recover" is doing real work.

## Summary

- Buy cheap-but-viable businesses, but only after a confirmed technical reversal.
- The reversal (higher low + close above a short MA) is the gate that avoids value traps.
- Stop below the reversal low; exit on trend break.
- Costs: false reversals, lateness, and the persistence of value traps.
