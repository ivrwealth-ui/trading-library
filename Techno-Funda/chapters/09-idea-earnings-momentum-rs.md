# 9. Idea 5 — Earnings Momentum + Relative Strength

## The market's favourite stocks, confirmed by both layers

This idea combines the *fundamental* signal of **earnings momentum** (profits accelerating, estimates rising) with the *technical* signal of **relative strength** (the stock outperforming the market). It targets the stocks that are improving on paper *and* being rewarded in price — the sweet spot of the whole framework.

## The idea

Two of the strongest documented tendencies in markets are:

1. **Earnings momentum** — companies whose earnings are *accelerating* (or whose estimates are being revised upward) tend to keep outperforming, because the market systematically under-reacts to improving fundamentals.
2. **Price momentum / relative strength** — stocks that are already *outperforming* tend to keep outperforming, because strength persists.

This idea requires **both at once**: the fundamentals must be *improving* (earnings momentum) and the price must be *confirming it* (relative strength). Either alone is weaker; together they are a strong, self-reinforcing signal — a stock whose fundamentals are improving *and* whose price is already leading.

## The fundamental screen (what)

- **Earnings momentum** — earnings per share *accelerating*: recent quarterly EPS growth faster than the trailing trend, or consecutive quarters of positive earnings surprises.
- **Estimate revisions** — analysts revising earnings estimates *upward* (a leading indicator of improving fundamentals).
- **Revenue confirmation** — top-line growth supporting the earnings improvement (earnings growth without revenue is lower-quality).

The screen identifies companies whose *fundamentals are on an improving trajectory right now* — not merely "good" or "cheap", but *getting better*.

## The technical trigger (when)

1. **Relative strength** — the stock is **outperforming the benchmark** (e.g., its trailing 3–6 month return exceeds NIFTY's, or its RS line is rising).
2. **Uptrend** — price above the rising 200-day MA.
3. **Entry** — a **breakout to a new high** or a **pullback that holds at a rising MA** (either of the standard triggers).
4. **Stop / exit** — below the breakout or pullback low; or exit when *either* the earnings momentum stalls (a major negative surprise) *or* the relative strength breaks down.

## A worked example (an NSE earnings-beater)

- A large-cap has posted three consecutive quarters of earnings *above* expectations, and analysts have revised estimates upward; revenue is growing in the high teens. It passes the earnings-momentum screen.
- Over the last six months, the stock has returned 28% versus NIFTY's 12% — **clear relative strength**, and it is above a rising 200-day MA.
- It **breaks out to a new 52-week high on volume**. **Entry**.
- The stock leads for the next two quarters as estimates keep rising; the investor trails the stop under the rising 20-day MA.
- A subsequent earnings report **misses** and the stock **closes below its 50-day MA**, breaking relative strength. **Exit**.

Result: the investor rode a stock that was improving *fundamentally* and *leading* on the chart — and exited when *both* signals deteriorated.

## Risks and limitations

- **Crowding.** The most obvious earnings-momentum + relative-strength names are also the most crowded; when they reverse, the reversal can be sharp (a crowded unwind).
- **Estimate-revision lag.** Estimate data can be stale or thin for smaller names; the signal is stronger for large, well-covered stocks.
- **Reversal after good news.** A stock can sell off *after* a great report ("buy the rumour, sell the news"); the relative-strength exit is what catches this.
- **Two-signal failure.** The idea exits when *either* layer breaks, which means it can exit early on a single bad quarter even if the longer trend is intact.

## Summary

- Combine earnings momentum (accelerating EPS, rising estimates) with relative strength (outperforming the market).
- Enter on breakout or pullback within an uptrend; exit when either the earnings momentum stalls or relative strength breaks.
- A self-reinforcing signal: improving fundamentals confirmed by leading price.
- Risks: crowding, data lag, sell-the-news, and two-signal exits.
