# 3. The Fundamental Screen

## Separating good businesses from the rest

The fundamental screen is the "what" half of techno-funda. Its job is to reduce the entire market to a shortlist of businesses worth owning. This chapter covers the four pillars of the screen — **quality, growth, valuation, and balance-sheet health** — and how to apply them in the Indian context.

## Pillar 1: Quality

A quality business earns a **high return on the capital it employs**, because that is the definition of a compounding machine.

| Metric | What it measures | Common screen |
|--------|------------------|---------------|
| ROE (Return on Equity) | Profit per unit of shareholder capital | ROE > 15% (sustained) |
| ROCE | Profit per unit of total capital employed | ROCE > 15% |
| Operating margin | Pricing power / efficiency | Stable or rising, > 10–15% |
| Consistent earnings | Reliability of the business | Positive EPS in most of the last 5–10 years |

The key word is **sustained**. A single year of high ROE is meaningless; five to ten years of it signals a durable advantage. A screen should look at the *average* over several years, not one quarter.

## Pillar 2: Growth

Quality tells you the business is good *now*; growth tells you it is getting better. Growth is what turns a good business into a rising stock over time.

- **Revenue growth** — is the top line expanding? (Look at 3–5 year CAGR.)
- **Earnings growth** — is profit growing at least as fast? (EPS CAGR.)
- **Consistency of growth** — is growth steady, or lumpy and unreliable?

A common screen: **revenue and EPS growing at 10–15%+ annually over 3–5 years**, with no major negative year. Growth quality matters more than raw speed — a 40% grower that crashes every other year is riskier than a steady 15% grower.

## Pillar 3: Valuation

A wonderful business can be a poor purchase at a silly price. Valuation is the **margin of safety**.

- **P/E (Price-to-Earnings)** — price relative to profit. Lower is cheaper, but compare *within* an industry, not across.
- **P/B (Price-to-Book)** — price relative to net assets; more relevant for asset-heavy businesses (banks, financials).
- **PEG (P/E ÷ growth rate)** — a rough value-for-growth measure; PEG near or below 1 is often considered reasonable.

The honest caveat: **valuation is not a timing tool.** A cheap stock can stay cheap, and an expensive quality stock can keep rising. In techno-funda, valuation is a *filter* (avoid paying absurd prices), while the *technical layer* handles timing. Valuation should be used to *exclude the clearly overpriced*, not to *pick the exact entry*.

## Pillar 4: Balance-sheet health

Growth and earnings mean little if the business is drowning in debt. Balance-sheet health is the survivability test.

- **Debt-to-equity** — leverage; a high ratio is a red flag (banks/financials are the exception, where leverage is the business model).
- **Interest coverage** — operating profit ÷ interest; must comfortably cover obligations.
- **Current ratio / working capital** — short-term liquidity.

A simple screen: **debt-to-equity below a threshold** (often 0.5–1.0 for non-financials), **interest coverage comfortably above 2×**, and a **positive operating cash flow** trend.

## Applying the screen in India

In the Indian market, a practical fundamental screen over the **NIFTY 500** (or a larger liquid universe) might look like:

1. **Quality:** ROE > 15% (5-year average), operating margin stable/rising.
2. **Growth:** revenue and EPS CAGR > 12% over 5 years.
3. **Valuation:** P/E below the stock's own 5-year median (or below a sector-relative ceiling).
4. **Health:** debt-to-equity < 1.0 (non-financials), interest coverage > 3×.

This is a *starting point*, not gospel — every threshold is adjustable to taste and sector. The point is not the specific numbers; it is that the screen is **explicit, written, and repeatable**, so the "what" decision is made systematically rather than by feel.

## The output: a universe, not a buy list

Crucially, the fundamental screen produces a **shortlist of candidates**, not a trade. Passing the screen means "worth owning at the right time." It does **not** mean "buy now." That decision belongs to the technical layer — the next chapter.

## Summary

- The fundamental screen has four pillars: quality, growth, valuation, and balance-sheet health.
- Quality = sustained high returns on capital (ROE/ROCE over years).
- Growth = steady revenue and EPS expansion (CAGR over 3–5 years).
- Valuation = margin of safety; a filter against overpaying, not a timing tool.
- Health = survivability (low leverage, high coverage, positive cash flow).
- The screen produces a candidate universe, not a buy signal.
