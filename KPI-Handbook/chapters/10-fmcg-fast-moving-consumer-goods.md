# 10. FMCG (Fast-Moving Consumer Goods)

**At a glance.** FMCG sells high-frequency, low-ticket branded staples — soaps, biscuits, oils, beverages, home care. The business runs on distribution reach and brand loyalty. Drivers are volume (population + consumption), pricing power (raw-material pass-through), and rural demand. Listed leaders include Hindustan Unilever, ITC, Nestlé India, Britannia, Dabur, and Marico.

## The KPIs

**Volume growth vs value growth** — the split between units sold and price/mix.

*Why it matters:* FMCG companies report both. Volume growth is the *true* demand signal; value growth can be flattered by price hikes. A company growing value on *declining volume* is pricing itself into demand destruction.

*How to read:* Healthy FMCG runs low-to-mid-single-digit volume growth in India (population + penetration). Read the volume/value gap every quarter.

*Source:* Quarterly earnings release and concall.

**Market share (per category)** — the company's share of each category it competes in (soap, shampoo, biscuits…).

*Why it matters:* In a distribution game, market share is the scoreboard — it tells you who is winning at the shelf.

*How to read:* Track share trends by category (reported in quarterly presentations). Share gains in the *core* categories matter more than in fringe launches.

*Source:* Quarterly investor presentation (companies disclose category share trends).

**Distribution reach (number of outlets)** — direct and indirect reach into kirana stores.

*Why it matters:* Distribution is the moat. Greater reach = more shelf space = more volume, and it is very hard for a competitor to replicate quickly.

*How to read:* Watch outlet additions and, increasingly, *rural reach*. A company expanding reach faster than peers is structurally gaining share.

*Source:* Annual report and investor presentations.

**Gross margin** — the buffer against raw-material cost (palm oil, crude derivatives, agri-commodities).

*Why it matters:* FMCG gross margins (40–60%+) are high but swing with input costs. The ability to *hold* gross margin through an input-cost cycle is pricing power in action.

*How to read:* A stable gross margin despite commodity spikes = strong pricing power; a collapsing gross margin = the brand cannot pass costs on.

*Source:* **Screener.in** and quarterly results.

**Advertising & promotion spend (A&P) as % of revenue** — marketing investment.

*Why it matters:* It is the fuel for brand moats, but overspending erodes margins. The ratio reveals brand-building intensity.

*How to read:* 6–12% of revenue is typical. A rising A&P that *doesn't* convert to market share is wasted spend; a falling A&P that *holds* share is a strong franchise.

*Source:* Annual report.

**Working-capital cycle** — receivable + inventory days − payable days.

*Why it matters:* FMCG is one of the few industries where *negative* working capital is a sign of strength — distributors pay upfront, inventory turns fast, and suppliers are paid late.

*How to read:* A negative or very low working-capital cycle is normal and good. A worsening cycle (rising inventory days) can flag slowing demand or channel stuffing.

*Source:* **Screener.in** and annual report.

**Rural vs urban growth** — the demand split.

*Why it matters:* Rural India is the volume growth engine and is more sensitive to monsoons, crop prices and inflation.

*How to read:* When rural growth outpaces urban, the sector is in a genuine demand upcycle; a rural slump (driven by weak monsoons or high food inflation) is the classic sector-wide headwind.

*Source:* Management commentary on concalls and sector reports.

## Healthy profile vs red flags

- **Healthy:** Volume growth positive, market share stable or rising, gross margin resilient, distribution reach expanding.
- **Red flags:** Value growth on negative volumes, share loss in core categories, channel stuffing (inventory building), or gross margin compression that lasts multiple quarters.
