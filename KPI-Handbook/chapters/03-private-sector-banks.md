# 3. Private Sector Banks

**At a glance.** A private bank earns by borrowing cheap (deposits) and lending dear (loans), pocketing the spread, minus the cost of loans that go bad. The entire business is a machine for converting deposits into performing loans. The key drivers are the credit cycle, the deposit franchise, and asset quality. Listed leaders include HDFC Bank, ICICI Bank, Kotak Mahindra, Axis Bank, and IndusInd.

## The KPIs

**Net Interest Margin (NIM)** — the core spread: `(Interest earned − Interest paid) ÷ Average interest-earning assets`. It measures how profitably the bank deploys its funds.

*Why it matters:* NIM is the bank's gross operating margin. A bank with a strong low-cost deposit base (CASA) funds itself cheaply and enjoys a structurally higher NIM.

*How to read:* Indian private banks typically run 3.5–4.5%. A rising NIM is good; a NIM that stays high *while* asset quality deteriorates is a warning that the bank is chasing risky yield.

*Source:* Quarterly results presentation and MD&A; also on **Screener.in** and **Moneycontrol** bank pages.

**CASA ratio** — the share of **Current + Savings Account** deposits in total deposits. These are the cheapest funds a bank can gather.

*Why it matters:* Low-cost deposits are the moat. A high CASA ratio directly supports a high NIM and buffers against rate cycles.

*How to read:* Private banks target 40–50%. A falling CASA ratio means the bank is increasingly funding itself with expensive term deposits, which compresses NIM.

*Source:* Quarterly investor presentation (bank always discloses CASA).

**Gross NPA / Net NPA ratio** — the share of loans that have stopped paying (gross) and net of provisions (net).

*Why it matters:* Asset quality is the single most important number for a bank — bad loans eat capital and profits for years.

*How to read:* Private banks typically hold gross NPA below 3% and net NPA below 1%. A spike in gross NPA, or a *shrinking gap* between gross and net NPA (provisions not keeping pace with bad loans), signals stress.

*Source:* Quarterly results and RBI's quarterly banking statistics; **Trendlyne** tracks NPA history.

**Provision Coverage Ratio (PCR)** — `Provisions held ÷ Gross NPAs`. It shows how much of the bad-loan book is already provided for.

*Why it matters:* A high PCR means the bank has already absorbed the pain; future recoveries flow straight to profit.

*How to read:* Strong private banks run PCR of 60–75%+. A falling PCR with rising NPA is a double negative.

*Source:* Quarterly presentation.

**Capital Adequacy Ratio (CRAR / CAR)** — `Capital ÷ Risk-weighted assets`, the regulatory buffer required by RBI.

*Why it matters:* A well-capitalised bank can grow lending without raising fresh equity (which dilutes shareholders).

*How to read:* Under Basel III the RBI minimum is 9% plus a 2.5% capital conservation buffer, i.e. ~11.5% (higher for banks designated systemically important). Strong banks run 15–18%+. Banks near the minimum are constrained or may need capital raises.

*Source:* Quarterly presentation and RBI.

**Cost-to-Income ratio** — `Operating expenses ÷ Operating income`. How much the bank spends to earn each rupee of income.

*Why it matters:* It reveals operating efficiency independent of the credit cycle.

*How to read:* Efficient private banks run 40–45%. A rising cost-to-income with a flat branch network suggests cost discipline is slipping.

*Source:* Quarterly presentation; **Screener.in** shows it historically.

**Credit growth & deposit growth** — the year-on-year growth of the loan book and the deposit base.

*Why it matters:* Loans are the earning engine; deposits are the fuel. A bank that lends faster than it gathers deposits is funding the gap with costly borrowings.

*How to read:* Watch the *spread* between the two. Credit growth well above deposit growth is a yellow flag. A healthy bank grows both in the 12–18% range in a normal cycle.

*Source:* RBI sectoral credit data and quarterly presentations.

## Healthy profile vs red flags

- **Healthy:** NIM ≥ 4%, CASA ≥ 45%, net NPA < 1%, PCR ≥ 60%, CRAR ≥ 15%, cost-to-income ≤ 45%.
- **Red flags:** CASA falling while NIM rising (yield-chasing), gross NPA rising for 2+ quarters, PCR falling, credit growth far outpacing deposit growth, or repeated capital raises.
