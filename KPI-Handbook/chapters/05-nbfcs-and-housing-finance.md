# 5. NBFCs and Housing Finance

**At a glance.** NBFCs (Non-Banking Financial Companies) lend like banks but cannot take deposits — they borrow from banks and the bond market and lend into niches banks underserve: vehicle finance, MSME, gold loans, microfinance, and housing (Housing Finance Companies, or HFCs). The business model is a spread business funded by wholesale borrowing, which makes the *liability side* as important as the asset side. Listed leaders include Bajaj Finance, Cholamandalam, and Shriram Finance; HFCs include LIC Housing Finance, PNB Housing, and Can Fin Homes. (HDFC Ltd, formerly the largest HFC, merged into HDFC Bank in 2023 and is no longer listed.)

## The KPIs

**AUM growth** — growth in Assets Under Management (the loan book).

*Why it matters:* For a lending franchise, AUM is the top line. Consistent AUM growth across cycles signals distribution strength and demand.

*How to read:* Compound AUM growth of 15–25% is healthy for a diversified NBFC. Watch that growth is not coming from a single hot segment (e.g. unsecured personal loans) at the cost of asset quality.

*Source:* Quarterly investor presentation.

**Net Interest Margin (NIM) / Spread** — the difference between lending yield and borrowing cost.

*Why it matters:* It is the NBFC's core profitability, but it must be read against risk — a very high NIM usually means riskier lending.

*How to read:* Diversified NBFCs run 6–9% NIM; HFCs 2–3.5%; gold-loan NBFCs higher. Compare NIM against the *same segment* peers, never across segments.

*Source:* Quarterly presentation and **Tijori Finance**.

**GNPA / NNPA and Provision Coverage Ratio** — asset quality and the buffer against it.

*Why it matters:* NBFC credit cycles are sharper than bank cycles because the borrower is typically a rung riskier and the funding is wholesale.

*How to read:* Diversified NBFCs hold gross NPA below 2–3% and net below 1%; microfinance runs structurally higher. A sudden spike in any retail NBFC is the earliest warning.

*Source:* Quarterly presentation and **Trendlyne**.

**Cost of funds** — the blended rate the NBFC pays on its borrowings.

*Why it matters:* Unlike banks, NBFCs have no cheap deposits, so their entire margin depends on borrowing cost. The ability to borrow cheaply is the franchise.

*How to read:* Falling cost of funds with rising or stable NIM is the best possible trend. Watch the *borrowing mix* — dependence on short-term commercial paper is a liquidity risk.

*Source:* Quarterly presentation and MD&A.

**Capital adequacy (CRAR) and leverage** — the capital buffer and `Total debt ÷ Net worth` gearing.

*Why it matters:* NBFCs grow by re-leveraging. Too much leverage leaves no cushion when the cycle turns.

*How to read:* RBI requires NBFCs to maintain ~15% CRAR. A comfortable NBFC holds 20%+ and keeps leverage below ~5–6×.

*Source:* Quarterly presentation and RBI.

**Collection efficiency** — the percentage of billed EMI dues actually collected in a period (key for vehicle, MFI and housing).

*Why it matters:* It is the earliest real-time health signal — a leading indicator that shows stress *before* it shows up in NPA ratios.

*How to read:* A stable 98–100% is normal for good franchises. A persistent drop to 95% or below flags trouble one or two quarters before NPA rises.

*Source:* Monthly business updates and quarterly presentations.

**For HFCs specifically — Loan-to-Value (LTV)** — the loan size relative to the property value.

*Why it matters:* It is the collateral cushion. Lower LTV = safer book (a 20% fall in property value still leaves the lender covered).

*How to read:* Prudent HFCs keep average LTV below 65–70%. High LTV (80%+) is a red flag in a housing downturn.

*Source:* HFC quarterly presentation.

## Healthy profile vs red flags

- **Healthy:** AUM compounding 15%+, NIM stable, net NPA < 2%, CRAR ≥ 20%, cost of funds falling, collection efficiency 98%+.
- **Red flags:** AUM growth funded by short-term borrowings, concentration in one segment, collection efficiency slipping, or leverage above 6×.
