# 27. Agrochemicals and Fertilizers

**At a glance.** This chapter covers two crop-input businesses: fertilizers (urea, DAP, complex — subsidized and regulated) and agrochemicals (crop-protection chemicals — herbicides, insecticides, fungicides). Both are tied to agriculture, monsoons and farmer income. Listed leaders: fertilizers — Coromandel International, Deepak Fertilisers, Chambal, National Fertilizers; agrochemicals — UPL, PI Industries, Dhanuka, Bayer CropScience (the Indian arm of a multinational).

## The KPIs — Fertilizers

**Plant utilization and production volume** — output relative to capacity.

*Why it matters:* Fertilizer plants are capital-intensive; utilization drives cost efficiency.

*How to read:* Watch production vs rated capacity. High utilization = efficiency; sustained low utilization signals feedstock or demand issues.

*Source:* Quarterly results and Ministry of Chemicals & Fertilizers.

**Gas cost and energy efficiency (Gcal/tonne)** — the feedstock cost and energy consumed per tonne of urea.

*Why it matters:* Natural gas is the dominant input for urea; energy efficiency (Gcal/tonne) is the technical moat, and gas cost is administered partly by government.

*How to read:* Lower energy consumption per tonne = better efficiency. Watch gas cost (pooled gas pricing) as the input driver.

*Source:* Annual report and industry data.

**Subsidy receivables** — money owed by the government on subsidized sales.

*Why it matters:* Urea and some fertilizers are sold at subsidized prices; the government reimburses the subsidy — but often late. Subsidy receivables are the cash-flow overhang.

*How to read:* Watch the level and ageing of subsidy receivables. Ballooning dues = working-capital strain even when the P&L looks healthy.

*Source:* Quarterly results.

**Urea vs non-urea (DAP/complex) mix** — the product split.

*Why it matters:* Urea is heavily regulated and low-margin; non-urea (DAP, complexes) and value-added products carry better economics.

*How to read:* A rising share of non-urea/complex and value-added (specialty) products is margin-positive.

*Source:* Quarterly presentation.

## The KPIs — Agrochemicals

**Revenue growth and monsoon dependence** — the weather-driven demand.

*Why it matters:* Agrochemical demand tracks the monsoon and crop acreage. A weak monsoon directly hits volumes.

*How to read:* Read a quarter against the monsoon performance and acreage. Weather is the exogenous driver; the company's job is to grow regardless via new products and exports.

*Source:* Quarterly results and IMD (monsoon) data.

**Product mix — herbicide/insecticide/fungicide** — the crop-protection split.

*Why it matters:* Different products have different margins and growth. Herbicides have been the fastest-growing segment; fungicides are higher-value.

*How to read:* Watch the mix shift toward higher-growth/higher-margin segments and the launch of new molecules.

*Source:* Annual report.

**New molecule launches and R&D** — the product pipeline.

*Why it matters:* Agrochemicals face generic competition; new patented/off-patent molecules and formulations are the growth and margin defence.

*How to read:* A steady cadence of new launches (with the R&D to back it) = sustainable growth. Watch the R&D-to-sales ratio.

*Source:* Annual report and investor presentations.

**Export share and capacity utilization** — the global business and plant usage.

*Why it matters:* Leading agrochemical firms (UPL, PI Industries) are global; exports diversify monsoon risk. Capacity utilization drives margins.

*How to read:* Rising export share with stable margins = global competitiveness. Watch the China-competition dynamic.

*Source:* Annual report and quarterly results.

## Healthy profile vs red flags

- **Healthy:** High utilization, energy-efficient plants, growing non-urea/value-added share, new molecule launches, low subsidy-receivable overhang.
- **Red flags:** Ballooning subsidy receivables, over-dependence on monsoon, thin product pipeline, or rising China generic competition eroding margins.
