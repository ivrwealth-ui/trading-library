# 26. Hotels and Tourism

**At a glance.** Hotels are the ultimate fixed-cost operating-leverage business: rooms, food & beverage (F&B), and banquets. Revenue per available room drives everything, and the industry is highly cyclical (tied to travel, business and tourism) but enjoys strong pricing power in upcycles. Listed leaders include Indian Hotels (Taj), EIH (Oberoi), Chalet, Lemon Tree, and Mahindra Holidays.

## The KPIs

**Occupancy rate** — the percentage of available rooms sold.

*Why it matters:* Occupancy is the volume metric of a hotel; with fixed costs, small occupancy changes drive large profit swings.

*How to read:* Indian branded hotels run 65–75% occupancy in good times. Compare with peers in the same city/segment.

*Source:* Quarterly results and industry trackers (HVS/STR).

**ARR (Average Room Rate)** — revenue per *occupied* room per night.

*Why it matters:* ARR is the pricing metric. It reflects brand positioning and pricing power.

*How to read:* Watch ARR trends with occupancy. A hotel that can raise ARR while holding occupancy has genuine pricing power.

*Source:* Quarterly results.

**RevPAR (Revenue Per Available Room)** — `Occupancy × ARR`, or `room revenue ÷ available rooms`. The master metric.

*Why it matters:* RevPAR combines occupancy and rate into the single number that drives room profitability — the most important KPI in hotels.

*How to read:* Rising RevPAR is the core positive signal; read whether it is driven by occupancy (volume) or ARR (price). Watch RevPAR against the pre-COVID peak for recovery tracking.

*Source:* Quarterly results (companies disclose RevPAR or its components).

**Room inventory and pipeline** — total rooms and rooms under development.

*Why it matters:* Hotels grow by adding rooms (owned or managed). The pipeline is the growth runway.

*How to read:* Watch room additions and the owned vs managed mix — asset-light (management contracts) is higher-return.

*Source:* Annual report.

**F&B and banquet revenue share** — revenue beyond rooms.

*Why it matters:* F&B and banquets/MICE (meetings, incentives, conferences, events) are the second revenue engine and can be higher-margin.

*How to read:* A rising F&B/banquet share diversifies revenue and boosts RevPAR-equivalent (total revenue per room).

*Source:* Annual report.

**Market mix — leisure vs business vs MICE** — the customer split.

*Why it matters:* Business and MICE travel is higher-yield; leisure is seasonal. The mix shapes revenue stability and pricing.

*How to read:* Watch the shift (e.g., post-COVID leisure-led recovery) and seasonality.

*Source:* Management commentary.

**EBITDA margin** — profitability (the operating-leverage output).

*Why it matters:* Hotel EBITDA margins expand dramatically in upcycles (operating leverage) — the sector's reward and its risk.

*How to read:* Margins of 30–40%+ at peak contrast with thin/negative margins in downturns. Read margin in the context of the cycle.

*Source:* **Screener.in** and quarterly results.

## Healthy profile vs red flags

- **Healthy:** RevPAR rising, ARR up with stable occupancy, growing room pipeline (asset-light), high EBITDA margin.
- **Red flags:** Occupancy falling, ARR cuts to fill rooms, heavy owned-room capex into a downturn, or thin margins despite high occupancy.
