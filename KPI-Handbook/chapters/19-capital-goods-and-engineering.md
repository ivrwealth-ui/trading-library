# 19. Capital Goods and Engineering

**At a glance.** Capital goods companies build the machines and equipment that other industries use — power equipment, industrial machinery, switchgear, defence, and more. The business is order-book-driven and cyclical, tied to the private and government capex cycle. Listed leaders include Siemens, ABB India, L&T, Thermax, Cummins, CG Power, and Bharat Electronics.

## The KPIs

**Order book** — the total value of orders yet to be executed (the backlog).

*Why it matters:* The order book is the *future revenue*. It gives multi-quarter visibility and is the single most-watched number in the sector.

*How to read:* A rising order book = revenue growth ahead. Watch the *quality* of the book (mix of slow government vs fast private orders) and whether it is executable.

*Source:* Quarterly results (companies disclose order book and inflows).

**Order inflows and book-to-bill ratio** — new orders won in the period, and `inflows ÷ revenue` for the period.

*Why it matters:* Inflows are the leading indicator; book-to-bill tells you whether the backlog is growing (>1) or shrinking (<1).

*How to read:* A sustained book-to-bill above 1 means the backlog is expanding. A few weak inflow quarters warn of slower revenue two to four quarters out.

*Source:* Quarterly results.

**Execution and working capital** — how fast the backlog converts to revenue, and the cash tied up in the process.

*Why it matters:* Capital goods projects are lumpy and payment terms are back-ended; execution speed and working-capital discipline determine cash conversion.

*How to read:* Watch receivable days and retention money. A rising receivable day count or stuck retention money flags collection problems, especially with government clients.

*Source:* **Screener.in** and annual report.

**EBITDA margin and project mix** — profitability and the type of work (product vs project, private vs government).

*Why it matters:* Product businesses carry higher margins than lumpy projects; government orders are often lower-margin and slower-paying.

*How to read:* Compare margins with the project mix. A shift toward products/private orders is margin-accretive. Watch for margin compression from competitive bidding on large tenders.

*Source:* Quarterly results and annual report.

**Tender wins / competitive intensity** — the firm's win rate on large contracts.

*Why it matters:* Winning the right tenders at the right price is the business; aggressive bidding to win (and then seeing margins erode) is the classic value-destroyer.

*How to read:* Watch margin trends *after* large tender wins — if wins come with falling margins, the firm is buying growth.

*Source:* Order announcements and concall.

## Healthy profile vs red flags

- **Healthy:** Rising order book and inflows, book-to-bill above 1, disciplined working capital, margins holding.
- **Red flags:** Book-to-bill below 1 for multiple quarters, rising receivables, margin erosion on new wins, or over-reliance on slow-paying government orders.
