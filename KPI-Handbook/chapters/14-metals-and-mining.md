# 14. Metals and Mining

**At a glance.** Metals (steel, aluminium, copper, zinc) are global commodity businesses: price-takers whose fortunes track global supply-demand and the domestic capex cycle. The lever a company controls is cost — raw-material security and conversion efficiency. Mining supplies the ore. Listed leaders: steel — Tata Steel, JSW Steel, Jindal Steel, SAIL; aluminium/copper — Hindalco, Vedanta; zinc — Hindustan Zinc; mining — Coal India, NMDC.

## The KPIs — Steel

**Realization per tonne** — average selling price per tonne of steel.

*Why it matters:* Steel is a price-taker; realization is set by the market, not the company. Tracking it separates *price* moves from *volume* and *cost* moves.

*How to read:* Compare realization against peers (regional and product mix differences). Read it *with* cost per tonne to get the spread (margin per tonne).

*Source:* Quarterly results and JPC (https://www.jpcindiansteel.nic.in) data.

**EBITDA per tonne** — operating profit per tonne.

*Why it matters:* The single cleanest profitability metric in steel — it isolates the margin per unit of steel, stripping out volume noise.

*How to read:* Track the cycle — EBITDA/tonne can swing from ₹3,000 to ₹15,000+ across the cycle. Compare against peers (those with integrated raw-material security sustain higher EBITDA/tonne).

*Source:* Quarterly investor presentation (companies disclose EBITDA/tonne).

**Cost per tonne and raw-material security (coking coal, iron ore)** — the input cost structure.

*Why it matters:* Coking coal is imported (price exposed to global markets); iron ore is available domestically but captive mines are the advantage. Raw-material integration is the moat.

*How to read:* Firms with captive iron-ore/coal mines (Tata Steel, JSW via acquisitions) sustain structurally lower cost per tonne. Watch the *spread* between realization and cost.

*Source:* Quarterly presentation and MD&A.

**Capacity utilization and production volume** — output as a % of capacity.

*Why it matters:* Steel has high fixed costs; utilization drives operating leverage.

*How to read:* Above 85% is healthy. A firm adding capacity into a downcycle compresses utilization and margins.

*Source:* Quarterly results and JPC.

**Product mix — flat vs long, value-added share** — the composition of the steel sold.

*Why it matters:* Value-added (coated, auto-grade, speciality) steel carries higher and more stable margins than commodity rebar.

*How to read:* A rising value-added share is a margin-quality upgrade. Watch the auto/construction end-market split.

*Source:* Quarterly presentation.

## The KPIs — Aluminium / Copper / Mining

**LME price and premium** — the global price (London Metal Exchange) plus regional premium.

*Why it matters:* Aluminium/copper/zinc prices are set globally; the company is a price-taker.

*How to read:* Track LME as the revenue driver, then judge the company on *cost* and *volume*.

*Source:* LME data via **Investing.com** / **TradingView**.

**Alumina cost and power cost** — the two biggest input costs for aluminium (alumina + electricity).

*Why it matters:* Aluminium's economics are dominated by alumina and power. Captive alumina and captive power are the cost moat.

*How to read:* Hindalco/Vedanta with captive alumina and captive power sustain lower cost per tonne than peers. Watch the cost curve position.

*Source:* Quarterly presentation.

**Smelter utilization and production volume** — output relative to capacity.

*Why it matters:* Smelting is continuous and fixed-cost; utilization is the operating-leverage lever.

*How to read:* Near-100% utilization is normal and healthy for smelters; downtime signals trouble.

*Source:* Quarterly results.

**Mining — ore production volume and grade, mine life** — for miners (Coal India, NMDC).

*Why it matters:* A miner's assets are its reserves; grade and mine life determine long-run value, while quarterly volume drives revenue.

*How to read:* Watch production volume vs targets, the ore grade trend (falling grade raises cost), and remaining mine life (a shrinking reserve is a terminal risk).

*Source:* Annual report and quarterly results; Indian Bureau of Mines (https://ibm.gov.in).

## Healthy profile vs red flags

- **Healthy:** EBITDA/tonne above peer average through the cycle, captive raw-material security, high utilization, rising value-added mix.
- **Red flags:** Cost per tonne rising faster than realization, aggressive capacity additions into a downcycle, or falling ore grades without new reserves.
