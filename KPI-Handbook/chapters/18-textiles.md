# 18. Textiles

**At a glance.** Textiles runs the full chain — fibre → yarn → fabric → garments — plus the branded/apparel end. India is a major cotton producer, giving it raw-material integration, and the industry is a large employer and exporter. Drivers are cotton prices, global apparel demand, and the shift of sourcing from China. Listed leaders include Arvind, Welspun, Vardhman Textiles, KPR Mill, Trident, and apparel plays like Raymond, Page Industries, and Aditya Birla Fashion and Retail.

## The KPIs

**Cotton price and inventory** — the raw-material cost and the stock carried.

*Why it matters:* Cotton is the dominant input and swings wildly. Companies that carry cotton inventory make or lose money on inventory valuation, separate from the operating business.

*How to read:* Watch the cotton-price trend and the company's inventory position. A rising cotton price inflates inventory value (one-time gains) but raises future input cost; falling cotton is the reverse. Strip inventory effects to see the operating margin.

*Source:* Quarterly results and Textile Ministry / industry data.

**Capacity utilization** — spinning/weaving output relative to capacity.

*Why it matters:* Textiles is fixed-cost heavy (spindles, looms); utilization drives margins.

*How to read:* Above 80% is healthy. Sustained low utilization signals weak demand or overcapacity.

*Source:* Quarterly presentation.

**Order book and export share** — forward orders and the share of revenue from exports.

*Why it matters:* Export orders (home textiles, garments) are the growth engine, and the order book gives visibility. Export share reflects global competitiveness.

*How to read:* Watch the order-book trend (especially home-textile exporters to the US) and the export/domestic mix. A rising export share with stable margins = gaining global share.

*Source:* Quarterly presentation and annual report.

**Product mix — yarn vs fabric vs garments, and value-added share** — the position in the value chain.

*Why it matters:* Each rung up the chain (yarn → fabric → garments) adds margin and stability. Branded garments are the highest-value end.

*How to read:* A firm moving up the value chain (more garments, more branded) is structurally improving its margin profile. Pure commodity yarn players have the thinnest, most cyclical margins.

*Source:* Annual report.

**Realization per unit** — average price per kg/metre of output.

*Why it matters:* It reflects product mix and pricing power.

*How to read:* Read realization with cotton cost (the spread). A stable spread with rising volume is the healthy signature.

*Source:* Quarterly results.

**For branded/apparel — store count, SSSG, brand strength** — the retail metrics of the branded end.

*Why it matters:* Branded apparel is a retail business; same-store sales and distribution drive it (see the Retail chapter).

*How to read:* Positive SSSG, expanding store/distribution network, and stable brand positioning are the key signals.

*Source:* Quarterly results and annual report.

## Healthy profile vs red flags

- **Healthy:** Cotton inventory managed well, high utilization, rising value-added/garment share, growing export order book.
- **Red flags:** Inventory losses from cotton swings, low utilization, dependence on a single export market, or a stuck-at-commodity-yarn position with no value-chain move.
