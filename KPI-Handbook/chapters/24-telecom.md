# 24. Telecom

**At a glance.** Telecom is an oligopoly of three private players (Reliance Jio, Bharti Airtel, Vodafone Idea) plus BSNL. After a brutal price war, the industry has consolidated and is now in a period of tariff repair. The business is a subscription + data game with heavy spectrum capex. Listed leaders: Bharti Airtel, Vodafone Idea (and Reliance Industries as the parent of Jio).

## The KPIs

**ARPU (Average Revenue Per User)** — monthly revenue per subscriber.

*Why it matters:* ARPU is the pricing barometer of the entire industry. Every rupee of ARPU growth drops almost directly to the bottom line (the cost base is largely fixed).

*How to read:* Indian ARPU bottomed near ₹120 around FY20 and has since been repaired through tariff hikes and the shift to 4G/5G; current levels differ by operator, so check the latest quarter's figure rather than a fixed number. Watch the ARPU trend, and the mix (2G → 4G/5G upgrade) that drives it.

*Source:* TRAI (https://www.trai.gov.in) and quarterly results.

**Subscriber base — gross adds, net adds, churn** — customers gained, net change, and those leaving.

*Why it matters:* Subscribers are the installed base; churn shows competitive intensity and service quality.

*How to read:* Watch net adds (gross minus churn) and the churn rate (typically 2–3% monthly). High churn = weak retention.

*Source:* TRAI and quarterly results.

**Data consumption per user (GB/user/month)** — usage intensity.

*Why it matters:* Data is the growth driver; rising consumption supports ARPU and network monetization.

*How to read:* Rising data per user with stable ARPU is the classic 4G/5G upgrade story.

*Source:* TRAI and quarterly results.

**4G/5G subscriber mix and spectrum holdings** — the technology mix and spectrum assets.

*Why it matters:* 5G is the next capex cycle; the subscriber mix shows upgrade progress, and spectrum holdings determine network quality and future obligations.

*How to read:* Watch 5G subscriber conversion and the spectrum-liability (deferred spectrum dues) burden on the balance sheet.

*Source:* Quarterly results and TRAI.

**Capex intensity** — capital spending on network rollout relative to revenue.

*Why it matters:* Telecom is capex-heavy (towers, spectrum, fibre); capex intensity determines free-cash-flow generation and leverage.

*How to read:* Falling capex intensity after a rollout cycle means free cash flow improves; a rising intensity (5G rollout) is a near-term cash drag.

*Source:* Quarterly results and annual report.

**For tower companies — tenancy ratio and tower count** — the number of tenants per tower (e.g. Indus Towers, in which Bharti Airtel is the largest shareholder).

*Why it matters:* Tenancy ratio (tenants per tower) is the margin lever for tower companies — each additional tenant adds revenue at near-zero marginal cost.

*How to read:* Rising tenancy ratio and stable tower count = operating leverage. Watch tenant concentration (dependence on a single operator like VIL is a receivable risk).

*Source:* Quarterly results.

## Healthy profile vs red flags

- **Healthy:** Rising ARPU, stable net adds, low churn, rising data usage, falling capex intensity after rollout.
- **Red flags:** ARPU falling (price war), high churn, subscriber loss, or ballooning spectrum/capex obligations without ARPU support.
