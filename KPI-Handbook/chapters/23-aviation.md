# 23. Aviation

**At a glance.** Aviation is a brutally capital-intensive, fuel-exposed, competition-prone business — India's market is effectively a duopoly between IndiGo (InterGlobe Aviation) and the Air India group (with SpiceJet and Akasa smaller). The KPIs are the classic airline unit-economics metrics. Listed leader: InterGlobe Aviation (IndiGo).

## The KPIs

**ASK (Available Seat Kilometres)** — total capacity deployed (`seats × kilometres flown`).

*Why it matters:* ASK is the supply side — the scale of the operation.

*How to read:* Watch ASK growth as the capacity story; airlines grow by adding planes and routes.

*Source:* DGCA (https://www.dgca.gov.in) monthly traffic data and quarterly results.

**RPK (Revenue Passenger Kilometres)** — `passengers × kilometres flown`. The demand side.

*Why it matters:* RPK measures paid demand. Growth in RPK relative to ASK determines load factor.

*Source:* DGCA and quarterly results.

**Passenger Load Factor (PLF)** — `RPK ÷ ASK`. How full the planes are.

*Why it matters:* Load factor is the single most watched operational metric — it shows whether capacity is being absorbed by demand.

*How to read:* Indian carriers run 85–90%+ load factors. A falling load factor (while ASK grows) means capacity is outpacing demand and fares will weaken.

*Source:* DGCA monthly data and quarterly results.

**Yield (₹/RPK)** — revenue per passenger-kilometre (the average fare).

*Why it matters:* Yield is the pricing metric. Load factor × yield = unit revenue (RASK).

*How to read:* Watch the load-factor/yield trade-off: airlines can fill planes by cutting fares (higher load, lower yield) or hold fares (lower load, higher yield). The best outcome is both rising.

*Source:* Quarterly results.

**CASK (Cost per ASK) and RASK (Revenue per ASK)** — unit cost and unit revenue; the margin per seat-kilometre is `RASK − CASK`.

*Why it matters:* These two unit metrics are the entire economics of an airline. Profitability is purely RASK beating CASK.

*How to read:* Watch the spread between RASK and CASK. CASK is dominated by fuel (a third or more) and is heavily exposed to crude and the rupee.

*Source:* Quarterly results.

**Fuel cost and fleet size/utilization** — the dominant cost and the aircraft assets.

*Why it matters:* Fuel is the swing factor in CASK. Fleet utilization (aircraft hours per day) drives cost per seat.

*How to read:* Higher fleet utilization lowers unit costs. Watch crude and rupee as the exogenous drivers of fuel CASK.

*Source:* Quarterly results and DGCA.

**Market share and on-time performance** — competitive position and service quality.

*Why it matters:* Market share shows who is winning the duopoly; on-time performance drives passenger preference and repeat business.

*How to read:* IndiGo has held ~60%+ domestic share; watch share shifts and punctuality as the competitive scoreboard.

*Source:* DGCA monthly traffic data.

## Healthy profile vs red flags

- **Healthy:** Load factor 85%+, RASK rising faster than CASK, high utilization, share stable or rising.
- **Red flags:** ASK growth far above RPK growth, falling load factor, fuel spike without fare pass-through, or RASK falling below CASK.
