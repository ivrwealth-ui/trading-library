# 9. Healthcare Services (Hospitals and Diagnostics)

**At a glance.** Hospitals and diagnostics are the *delivery* side of healthcare — capital-intensive bed-and-equipment businesses whose unit economics are measured per bed, per patient and per test. Drivers are medical inflation, insurance penetration, and capacity expansion. Listed leaders: hospitals — Apollo, Max, Fortis, Narayana; diagnostics — Dr Lal PathLabs, Metropolis, Vijaya Diagnostic.

## The KPIs — Hospitals

**ARPOB (Average Revenue Per Occupied Bed)** — `Hospital revenue ÷ Occupied bed-days`. The revenue earned per occupied bed per day. Companies differ on what counts as revenue (some include outpatient income, some exclude pharmacy), so compare only like-for-like definitions.

*Why it matters:* It is the price metric of a hospital — the blend of specialty mix and pricing power.

*How to read:* Rising ARPOB is good *if* driven by a better specialty/payor mix (cardiology, oncology, transplants) rather than by raising rack rates on a static mix. Compare ARPOB with peers in the same city.

*Source:* Quarterly investor presentation.

**Occupancy rate** — `Occupied bed-days ÷ Available bed-days`.

*Why it matters:* It is the utilization metric of the fixed-cost bed base; hospitals have high fixed costs, so occupancy drives operating leverage.

*How to read:* 65–75% is typical for mature hospitals; new hospitals run lower during ramp-up. Watch occupancy on the *mature* bed base (excluding newly added beds that are still ramping).

*Source:* Quarterly presentation.

**ALOS (Average Length of Stay)** — the average number of days a patient stays.

*Why it matters:* It measures throughput efficiency and, indirectly, case mix (complex cases stay longer).

*How to read:* A falling ALOS can be good (efficiency) or bad (loss of complex/surgical cases). Read ALOS *with* ARPOB: ALOS up and ARPOB up = better case mix; ALOS down and ARPOB down = losing complexity.

*Source:* Quarterly presentation.

**Bed capacity and additions** — total operational beds and newly added beds.

*Why it matters:* Hospitals grow by adding beds; the growth pipeline is visible years ahead in announced expansion.

*How to read:* Track beds added vs occupancy — adding beds into a saturated market dilutes occupancy and margins. A hospital adding beds *while* holding occupancy is compounding well.

*Source:* Annual report and quarterly presentation.

**Payor mix** — the share of revenue from cash/self-pay vs insurance/TPA vs government schemes.

*Why it matters:* Insurance and self-pay reimburse better than government schemes (like CGHS/Ayushman), so payor mix drives realization.

*How to read:* A rising share of cash + insurance (versus government schemes) is margin-positive.

*Source:* Annual report and investor presentations.

## The KPIs — Diagnostics

**Test volume growth and realization per test** — the number of tests and the average revenue per test.

*Why it matters:* Diagnostics is a volume-and-price game: growth must come from both more tests and stable pricing.

*How to read:* Watch the split — volume growth with *stable* realization is healthy; a price war (realization falling) is the sector's biggest risk, especially in the fragmented pathology market.

*Source:* Quarterly investor presentation.

**Network size — labs and collection centres** — the physical footprint.

*Why it matters:* Diagnostics is a network-density business; collection centres feed the central labs and drive walk-in volume.

*How to read:* Track centre additions and their productivity (revenue per centre). Rapid centre additions with falling per-centre revenue mean low-quality expansion.

*Source:* Quarterly presentation and annual report.

**Revenue per patient** — the average bill per patient.

*Why it matters:* A proxy for test mix and cross-selling of higher-value (specialized) tests.

*How to read:* Rising revenue per patient is good when driven by specialized/preventive test mix, not price hikes alone.

*Source:* Annual report.

## Healthy profile vs red flags

- **Healthy:** Hospitals — occupancy 65%+, ARPOB rising with case mix, payor mix improving. Diagnostics — double-digit volume growth, stable realization, productive network.
- **Red flags:** Occupancy falling on a mature bed base, aggressive bed additions into saturated markets, or diagnostic realization falling in a price war.
