# 17. Chemicals and Petrochemicals

**At a glance.** Chemicals divides into commodity chemicals (price-takers, driven by global spreads) and specialty chemicals (differentiated, driven by product mix and customer relationships). India's specialty-chemical sector has been a multi-year structural growth story on the "China+1" sourcing shift. Listed leaders: specialty — SRF, Aarti Industries, Vinati Organics, Deepak Nitrite, Navin Fluorine; commodity — Tata Chemicals, UPL (also agrochemicals, see its chapter), Deepak Fertilisers.

## The KPIs

**Product mix — specialty vs commodity share** — the split of revenue.

*Why it matters:* Specialty products carry higher, more stable margins and customer stickiness; commodity products are cyclical and margin-thin. The mix determines the quality of the business.

*How to read:* A rising specialty share is a structural upgrade. Compare across peers in the *same* chemistry.

*Source:* Annual report and quarterly presentation.

**Capacity utilization** — output as a % of installed capacity.

*Why it matters:* Chemical plants are capital-intensive and continuous; utilization drives operating leverage.

*How to read:* Above 80% is healthy. Watch utilization alongside announced capex — new capacity that is not absorbed compresses margins.

*Source:* Quarterly presentation and annual report.

**Capex pipeline and project execution** — planned and ongoing capacity expansion.

*Why it matters:* Specialty-chemical growth is built project-by-project. The capex pipeline is the visible future growth; execution (on-time, on-budget) determines returns.

*How to read:* Track announced projects, their timelines and cost overruns. A firm with a deep project pipeline and a clean execution record compounds best.

*Source:* Annual report and concall.

**Spread (product price − raw-material cost)** — the margin spread for commodity products.

*Why it matters:* For commodity chemicals, the *spread* is the profit; it is driven by global supply-demand. Tracking the spread separates the company's cost skill from market luck.

*How to read:* Watch the spread trend for the key product (e.g., soda ash, caustic). A widening spread = favourable cycle; a narrowing spread compresses margins.

*Source:* Industry price trackers and management commentary.

**Export share and customer diversification** — revenue from exports and the customer base.

*Why it matters:* Specialty-chemical growth is export-led (global pharma/agro clients). Export share reflects global competitiveness, and customer diversification reduces concentration risk.

*How to read:* Rising export share with stable margins = gaining global share. Watch customer concentration — a few large customers is a risk if one shifts sourcing.

*Source:* Annual report.

**EBITDA margin** — profitability (specialty margins are structurally higher than commodity).

*Why it matters:* It separates the quality of the franchise. Specialty players sustain 18–28% EBITDA margins; commodity players swing with the cycle.

*How to read:* A specialty firm whose margin keeps *rising* is likely improving mix or scale; a commodity firm's margin tells you where you are in the cycle.

*Source:* **Screener.in** and quarterly results.

## Healthy profile vs red flags

- **Healthy:** Rising specialty mix, high utilization, deep well-executed capex pipeline, diversified customers.
- **Red flags:** Commodity spread narrowing without cost relief, project delays/cost overruns, customer concentration, or capacity added into a downcycle.
