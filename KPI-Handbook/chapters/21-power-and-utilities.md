# 21. Power and Utilities

**At a glance.** Power splits into generation (thermal, hydro, renewables), transmission, and distribution. It is a regulated, capital-intensive utility business with steady but low-return economics — the quality of the franchise depends on fuel security, offtake agreements and payment discipline from state discoms. Listed leaders: generation — NTPC, Tata Power, Adani Power, JSW Energy; transmission — Power Grid; distribution/utilities — Torrent Power, CESC; renewables — NTPC Green, Adani Green (ReNew is listed in the US, not on NSE/BSE).

## The KPIs

**Plant Load Factor (PLF)** — actual generation as a % of maximum possible generation.

*Why it matters:* PLF is the utilization metric of a power plant — the single most important number for generation economics.

*How to read:* Thermal PLFs of 60–85% are typical; anything sustained below ~55% signals weak offtake. Watch PLF trends with fuel availability. Renewable plants report **CUF (Capacity Utilization Factor)** instead.

*Source:* CEA (https://cea.nic.in) and quarterly results.

**Installed capacity and capacity mix** — total generation capacity and the thermal/hydro/renewable split.

*Why it matters:* The mix determines the growth story — renewables are the structural growth driver; thermal faces the energy-transition overhang.

*How to read:* Watch renewable capacity additions and the share of regulated vs merchant capacity (merchant = market-price exposure).

*Source:* CEA and quarterly results.

**Fuel cost and fuel security** — the cost and assured supply of coal/gas.

*Why it matters:* Fuel is the largest cost for thermal plants; assured fuel (captive mines, long-term coal linkages) is the moat.

*How to read:* Watch coal linkage and captive-mine coverage. Plants dependent on expensive imported coal are margin-vulnerable.

*Source:* Quarterly results and MD&A.

**Realization per unit and PPA vs merchant mix** — the price received per kWh and the share of power sold under long-term Power Purchase Agreements vs the open market.

*Why it matters:* PPAs give stable, regulated returns; merchant power earns market (volatile) rates. The mix determines risk and return.

*How to read:* A high PPA share = stable cash flows; a high merchant share = cyclical upside and risk. Watch the average realization per unit.

*Source:* Quarterly results.

**Receivables from discoms (dues)** — money owed by state distribution companies.

*Why it matters:* State discoms are chronically slow payers; ballooning dues are the sector's structural risk and a major cash-flow drag.

*How to read:* Watch the level and age of discom receivables. Rising, ageing dues are a red flag even when the plant is profitable on paper.

*Source:* PRAAPTI portal (Ministry of Power) and quarterly results.

**AT&C losses and collection efficiency (distribution)** — Aggregate Technical & Commercial losses and the share of billed amount collected.

*Why it matters:* For distribution utilities, AT&C losses (theft + technical + billing inefficiency) and collection efficiency determine profitability.

*How to read:* Falling AT&C losses and rising collection efficiency are the turnaround signature of a distribution franchise. National averages have historically been ~15–20% AT&C losses.

*Source:* UDAY/PRAAPTI portal and quarterly results.

## Healthy profile vs red flags

- **Healthy:** High PLF/CUF, secure fuel, high PPA share, low/falling discom dues.
- **Red flags:** Falling PLF, merchant-heavy with volatile fuel, ballooning discom receivables, or high AT&C losses in distribution.
