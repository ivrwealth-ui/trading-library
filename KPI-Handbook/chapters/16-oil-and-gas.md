# 16. Oil and Gas

**At a glance.** Oil & gas spans the value chain: upstream (exploration & production), downstream (refining & marketing), and gas transmission and distribution (pipelines and city gas). Each segment has its own KPIs. Listed leaders: upstream — ONGC, Oil India; refining/marketing — Reliance, BPCL, HPCL, IOC; gas — GAIL, IGL, MGL, Gujarat Gas; city gas — Indraprastha Gas, Mahanagar Gas.

## The KPIs — Upstream (E&P)

**Crude realization ($/barrel)** — the price received for produced crude.

*Why it matters:* Upstream revenue is `production × price`. Realization tracks global crude with some discount (Indian crude is often sold at a small discount to Brent).

*How to read:* Read realization with production. Rising crude = revenue tailwind; the company has little control over price, so judge it on production and costs.

*Source:* Quarterly results and PPAC (https://www.ppac.gov.in).

**Production volume (oil & gas)** — barrels/units produced.

*Why it matters:* The volume engine. Declining mature fields are the sector's secular challenge.

*How to read:* Watch production trends vs guidance; a field entering decline without new discoveries is a long-run negative.

*Source:* Quarterly results.

## The KPIs — Refining & Marketing

**Gross Refining Margin (GRM, $/barrel)** — the refining spread: `value of refined products − cost of crude`, before the refinery's operating costs (which is why it differs from a net refining margin).

*Why it matters:* GRM is the profitability engine of a refiner — a measure of how much value the refinery adds per barrel. It is set by global product spreads (diesel/gasoline crack spreads) and the refinery's complexity.

*How to read:* Complex refineries (like Reliance's) sustain structurally higher GRM. Track GRM against the Singapore benchmark margin. Falling GRM = weak product demand or oversupply.

*Source:* Quarterly results and management commentary.

**Throughput and capacity utilization** — crude processed relative to capacity.

*Why it matters:* Refineries run near full capacity; throughput drives revenue and spreads fixed costs.

*How to read:* Above 95–100% is normal. Turnarounds (planned maintenance) temporarily lower throughput — distinguish these from demand-driven cuts.

*Source:* Quarterly results and PPAC.

**Inventory gains/losses** — the effect of crude-price moves on the value of stock in hand.

*Why it matters:* When crude rises, refiners book inventory gains; when it falls, losses. These are *non-operating* and distort quarterly profits.

*How to read:* Always strip inventory gains/losses to see the *underlying* GRM. A quarter boosted only by inventory gains is lower-quality.

*Source:* Management commentary on concalls.

**Marketing margin and retail volumes** — the margin and volume on fuel retailing (petrol/diesel sales).

*Why it matters:* Marketing margins are administered and cyclical; retail volumes track economic activity and vehicle usage.

*How to read:* Watch retail volume growth as a demand proxy, and margin stability (which can be compressed when the government freezes retail prices while crude rises).

*Source:* PPAC and quarterly results.

## The KPIs — Gas / City Gas Distribution

**Gas transmission volume and utilization** — gas transported through pipelines.

*Why it matters:* GAIL and pipeline players earn on volume through their network; utilization reflects network efficiency.

*How to read:* Watch volume growth and the mix of domestic vs imported (LNG) gas — LNG is pricier and its availability drives costs.

*Source:* Quarterly results and PPAC.

**CNG / PNG volumes and connections** — compressed natural gas (vehicles) and piped natural gas (households) sold.

*Why it matters:* City gas distributors (IGL, MGL) grow by adding CNG stations and PNG household connections; volumes are the top line.

*How to read:* Track CNG volume (tied to vehicles) and PNG connections (tied to new construction). Watch the spread between the gas cost and selling price — city gas margins are partly regulated.

*Source:* Quarterly results and PNGRB data.

## Healthy profile vs red flags

- **Healthy:** Upstream — stable or rising production, cost control. Refining — high GRM, near-full throughput. Gas — volume and connection growth with stable margins.
- **Red flags:** Relying on inventory gains for profit, falling throughput, declining field production, or margin compression from price regulation.
