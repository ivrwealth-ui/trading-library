# 30. Sugar and Paper

**At a glance.** Sugar and paper are both agro-processing businesses with cyclical commodity economics. Sugar is uniquely shaped by government policy (cane pricing, exports, ethanol blending); paper rides the packaging and printing cycle. Listed leaders: sugar — Balrampur Chini, Triveni, Dwarikesh, EID Parry; paper — JK Paper, West Coast Paper, Tamil Nadu Newsprint (TNPL), Century.

## The KPIs — Sugar

**Cane crushed and recovery rate** — the volume of sugarcane processed and the sugar extracted per tonne (`sugar produced ÷ cane crushed`).

*Why it matters:* Cane crushed is the top line; recovery rate is the technical efficiency. Higher recovery = more sugar (and more revenue) from the same cane.

*How to read:* Watch both. A rising recovery rate is a genuine efficiency moat; falling recovery (crop quality) hurts output.

*Source:* Quarterly results and annual report.

**Sugar realization and cane cost (FRP/SAP)** — the selling price and the administered cane price.

*Why it matters:* The margin is the spread between sugar realization and cane cost, and cane cost is *set by the government* (Fair & Remunerative Price, or State-Advised Price) — the company has no control over it. This is the sector's defining constraint.

*How to read:* Watch the spread. Rising cane prices (SAP) that outpace sugar realization compress margins — the recurring policy risk.

*Source:* Quarterly results and government notifications.

**Ethanol blending and distillery capacity** — the diversification into ethanol.

*Why it matters:* Ethanol (blended into petrol) is the structural growth story and margin stabilizer for sugar mills — it uses cane juice/molasses and is sold at government-set prices. Distillery capacity is the growth asset.

*How to read:* Watch ethanol volumes, distillery capacity additions and the blending target (the 20% "E20" programme, which the government targeted for 2025-26; check the latest achieved blending level and any successor roadmap). Higher ethanol share reduces dependence on cyclical sugar prices.

*Source:* Quarterly results and government (Ministry of Petroleum) data.

**By-product revenue (bagasse, molasses)** — revenue from co-products.

*Why it matters:* Bagasse (for power) and molasses (for ethanol/alcohol) add revenue and stabilize margins.

*How to read:* Watch the co-product contribution; integrated mills with power + distillery are more resilient.

*Source:* Quarterly results.

## The KPIs — Paper

**Capacity utilization and volume** — output relative to capacity.

*Why it matters:* Paper is capital-intensive; utilization drives cost per tonne.

*How to read:* Above 80% is healthy. Watch volume by segment (packaging vs writing & printing).

*Source:* Quarterly results.

**Raw-material cost — wood pulp vs waste paper** — the input cost.

*Why it matters:* Paper's cost is dominated by fibre (wood pulp or recycled waste paper). Waste-paper prices swing with imports; wood cost depends on plantation access.

*How to read:* Watch the input cost trend and whether the firm is backward-integrated into plantations (cost moat).

*Source:* Annual report.

**Realization per tonne and product mix** — the selling price and the segment split.

*Why it matters:* Packaging paper (kraft/board) is the growth, higher-margin segment; writing & printing (W&P) paper is in structural decline (digital shift).

*How to read:* Watch the shift toward packaging and the realization per tonne. A firm stuck in W&P faces secular headwinds.

*Source:* Quarterly results.

## Healthy profile vs red flags

- **Healthy:** Sugar — high recovery, integrated distillery (ethanol), stable spread despite cane costs. Paper — high utilization, backward-integrated fibre, rising packaging share.
- **Red flags:** Sugar — rising SAP squeezing the spread, no distillery diversification. Paper — dependence on W&P paper, unhedged waste-paper imports, low utilization.
