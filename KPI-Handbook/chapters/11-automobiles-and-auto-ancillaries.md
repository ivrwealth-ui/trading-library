# 11. Automobiles and Auto Ancillaries

**At a glance.** Automobiles is a cyclical, capital-intensive business driven by consumer income, financing cost and the product cycle. OEMs (Original Equipment Manufacturers) design and sell vehicles; ancillaries supply components. Vehicle sales data is the most transparent in India — published monthly by SIAM. Listed OEM leaders: Maruti Suzuki, Tata Motors (whose passenger- and commercial-vehicle businesses were separated into distinct listed companies in 2025 — check current names), M&M, Bajaj Auto, Hero MotoCorp, Eicher, Ashok Leyland. Ancillaries: Samvardhana Motherson, Bosch, Bharat Forge, Sona Comstar, Uno Minda.

## The KPIs — OEMs

**Volume growth (domestic + exports)** — units sold, split domestic and exports.

*Why it matters:* Volume is the top line of an auto OEM and the most transparent data in India (monthly SIAM + company filings).

*How to read:* Compare the company's growth to the *industry* growth — share gain/loss shows up immediately. Track monthly volumes to catch inflection points early.

*Source:* SIAM (https://www.siam.in) and company monthly sales disclosures.

**Market share (by segment)** — share of passenger vehicles, two-wheelers, commercial vehicles etc.

*Why it matters:* Share is the competitive scoreboard, and it is unusually transparent in autos.

*How to read:* A stable or rising share in the core segment is the hallmark of a strong OEM; sustained share loss (even with growing volumes) signals product-cycle weakness.

*Source:* SIAM monthly data.

**Capacity utilization** — production as a % of installed capacity.

*Why it matters:* Autos is fixed-cost heavy; utilization drives operating leverage and margins.

*How to read:* Above ~80% is healthy; sustained low utilization (below ~65%) means the OEM is over-capitalized and margins will suffer. Watch utilization *with* expansion plans.

*Source:* Annual report and management commentary.

**Dealer inventory days** — the number of days of vehicle stock sitting with dealers.

*Why it matters:* It is the leading indicator of the demand-supply balance. A build-up of dealer inventory means retail demand is softer than wholesale dispatches (channel stuffing).

*How to read:* 20–30 days is healthy for cars; 4–6 weeks is normal for two-wheelers. Inventory spiking above normal signals an impending production cut or discounting.

*Source:* FADA (Federation of Automobile Dealers Associations) data and management commentary.

**Average Selling Price (ASP) / realization per vehicle** — revenue per unit sold.

*Why it matters:* It reveals the mix shift — a rising ASP means the company is selling more premium vehicles (SUVs, EVs), which is margin-accretive.

*How to read:* ASP rising alongside volume = premiumization working. ASP falling = mix dilution or discounting.

*Source:* Computed from revenue ÷ volumes; management commentary.

**Product mix — SUV vs hatchback, EV share, etc.** — the composition of sales.

*Why it matters:* Premium and EV segments carry higher margins and are the growth direction of the market.

*How to read:* Watch the EV share trajectory and the SUV mix. A firm with a weak EV pipeline risks secular share loss even if current volumes look fine.

*Source:* SIAM and company disclosures.

## The KPIs — Ancillaries

**Content per vehicle** — the value of components supplied per vehicle.

*Why it matters:* Ancillaries grow faster than the underlying vehicle industry when they *increase content per vehicle* (more electronics, safety, EV components).

*How to read:* A supplier growing revenue faster than OEM volumes is increasing content — the key structural growth lever. EV transition is a content opportunity *and* a threat to combustion-specific parts.

*Source:* Annual report and analyst calls.

**Customer concentration** — dependence on the largest OEM clients.

*Why it matters:* Concentrated customers mean concentrated risk; diversified ones smooth out individual OEM cycles.

*How to read:* Watch the trend. Heavy dependence on one OEM (or one platform) is a risk if that OEM loses share.

*Source:* Annual report.

**Export share** — the share of revenue from exports.

*Why it matters:* Exports diversify away from domestic OEM cycles and, for some, command better margins.

*How to read:* Rising export share with stable margins is positive. Watch currency and global auto-cycle exposure.

*Source:* Annual report.

**EBITDA margin and capital intensity** — profitability and the capex required per rupee of growth.

*Why it matters:* Ancillary margins (8–15%) are thinner than OEMs in some cases but more stable; capital intensity determines return on capital.

*How to read:* Compare margins with peers in the *same* product line (casting, forging, wiring, etc.), not across all ancillaries.

*Source:* **Screener.in** and annual report.

## Healthy profile vs red flags

- **Healthy:** OEM — volume growth above industry, share stable, utilization 80%+, dealer inventory normal. Ancillary — content per vehicle rising, diversified customers, margins stable.
- **Red flags:** Dealer inventory ballooning, sustained share loss, ASP falling on discounting, or an ancillary overly dependent on one OEM.
